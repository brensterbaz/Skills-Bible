# Brief to Script

Turn the user’s source material into a clear first-draft script without pretending the draft is final or inventing supporting evidence.

## Establish the brief

Identify these fields from the request:

- audience;
- one message the audience should leave with;
- intended format and approximate length;
- tone and language;
- points that must be included;
- claims, topics, or wording to avoid;
- next step or call to action.

When a non-critical field is missing, make a reasonable assumption and state it briefly. Ask one concise question only when the missing answer would materially change the script, such as an unclear audience or conflicting goal.

Preserve the user’s facts, examples, required wording, and hard constraints. Treat supplied reference material as evidence, not as a structure that must be copied in order.

## Write the script

Choose the simplest structure that fits the job. A useful default for a short explainer is:

1. open with the audience’s situation or desired result;
2. explain one central idea in plain language;
3. make the idea concrete with the supplied example or mechanism;
4. close with the requested next step.

Write for the ear rather than the page:

- use natural sentences and contractions;
- keep one idea in each sentence or short paragraph;
- remove throat-clearing, generic scene-setting and repeated conclusions;
- prefer concrete verbs over abstract claims;
- match the user’s language and locale;
- keep the call to action proportionate to the brief.

Do not add statistics, testimonials, savings, performance claims, product capabilities, or guarantees that are not supported by the source material. When an unsupported claim is important to the requested script, flag it for evidence instead of smoothing it into the copy.

## Deliver

Return:

1. **Assumptions** — only when you had to infer something material;
2. **Script** — one clean, editable spoken-word draft;
3. **Review notes** — word count, any evidence needed, and any wording that deserves human review.

Treat a requested duration as a target. Do not claim measured timing unless audio has actually been recorded or read aloud and timed.

Stop at the script. Do not create a storyboard, shot list, visual design, audio, animation, or rendered media unless the user separately asks for that work.
